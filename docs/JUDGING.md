# What we're judged on

## Today (devkick 005, 5pm presentations)
No published criteria. What the organizers show they value:
- "Demo or it didn't happen": it runs live, no excuses.
- "Ship a playable game."
- Their guide: grey boxes first and get it fun, then Meshy models; asset manifest; tests; a PR per
  working milestone; "Built with Devin" credit; deployed to a public link.
- Their example games (devkick.com/downhill, devkick.com/floor13) use every sponsor tool visibly; Downhill
  has a live AI commentator.

## Later (Chroma Awards, Arcade & Party)
Four equal criteria, 1-10 each:
1. **Theme & Division Purpose**: "anyone can enjoy within seconds"; fast, replayable, social; quick
   rounds, friendly competition, strong game feel.
2. **Creativity & AI Innovation**: fresh idea; AI used in a new way, ideally in the gameplay.
3. **Craft & Technical Polish**: game feel, mechanics, art direction, no bugs.
4. **Sound & Emotional Impact**: audio quality, and does it make people feel something.
"AI is not why a project wins." Tool awards (Meshy, ElevenLabs) need significant use + tags + proof.

## Rules that can disqualify us
Public web link, no download. English text or subtitles. Original or licensed content only (no other
games' characters, brands, deepfakes, unlicensed music). Nothing that wouldn't be OK on YouTube.

## Jam judges' scoring sheet (shown on screen at the jam, 4:01pm Nov 14)
Each scored 1-5, times its weight, total out of 100:
1. **Core idea (x5)**: explain it in one sentence; does it sound fun?
2. **Creativity (x4)**: fresh concept, or a familiar genre with a real twist?
3. **Player experience (x4)**: do you understand what the player does and feels (demo, mockup or walkthrough)?
4. **Sound and voice (x4)**: is audio core to the experience? A clip helps; a precise plan scores the same.
5. **Path to finished (x3)**: realistic scope, clear next step toward a Chroma entry by Jan 7?

Specific beats vague: "spooky sounds" is a 2; "a heartbeat that speeds up as the monster gets closer, and a
narrator who taunts you after each death" is a 4-5 (5 if built).
Tie-breakers: 1) which game we'd most want to play, 2) most ambitious audio, 3) most progress for team size.
Questions they'll ask: "What does the player do in the first 30 seconds?", "What does it sound like?",
"What's the one thing you'd build next?"
